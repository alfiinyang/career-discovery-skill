# Impact

## 1. Meaning

**Impact is the outcome, effect, or change a person wants their activity to produce.** It describes why the activity matters to them or what they hope will be different because of it.

Impact may concern individuals, communities, organizations, systems, knowledge, culture, or the person's own life.

Examples:
- Helping learners understand difficult concepts.
- Making a process safer or less expensive.
- Expanding access to reliable information.
- Producing new knowledge about how people behave.
- Creating a sustainable livelihood or greater independence.

Impact can be personal or societal, immediate or long-term, direct or indirect.

## 2. What Impact Is Not

### Not the activity itself
“Build an educational app” is a vision. “Help learners practice mathematics independently” is an intended impact.

An activity can produce several outcomes, and an outcome may be achieved through many different activities.

### Not automatically a grand social mission
Impact does not have to be world-changing or altruistic. A person may want to earn a living, support family, gain independence, make a useful product, satisfy curiosity, or improve a small part of their environment.

Personal outcomes are legitimate impact statements when they describe what the person wants their efforts to accomplish.

### Not a guaranteed result
Impact expresses an intended or hoped-for outcome, not proof that the activity will achieve it. Actual effects may differ from intended effects.

### Not the same as motivation in every respect
Impact is the desired consequence. Motivation can also include enjoyment, identity, recognition, belonging, security, or the experience of doing the activity itself.

### Not evidence of capacity
Wanting to improve public health does not establish the person's ability to create a health intervention or produce that outcome.

## 3. How Impact Is Recognized

Impact is often expressed through a change or result:
- Who or what the person hopes to help or affect.
- A problem or condition they want to improve.
- Something they want to make possible, easier, safer, fairer, more accessible, or better understood.
- A personal condition they want their work to create, such as independence or financial stability.
- A form of knowledge, experience, or value they want to produce.

Impact may be:
- **Defined:** The desired outcome is sufficiently articulated.
- **Partially defined:** There are clues or broad intentions, but the outcome is not yet clear.
- **Undefined:** The person does not currently know what they want the activity to accomplish.
- **Contradictory:** Stated outcomes conflict or do not yet reconcile.
- **Insufficiently evidenced:** The stated outcome is too vague or ungrounded to interpret confidently.

“I don't know” is a valid current state. It is not evidence that the person lacks purpose, ambition, or values. It may reflect limited exposure, reflection, or experience.

## 4. Relationship to the Other Pillars

### Impact and Vision
Impact describes the intended outcome; vision describes the activity intended to produce it.

The connection may be clear, uncertain, or weak. A person may want a particular outcome but propose an activity that does not obviously lead to it. That gap is information, not automatically proof that either statement is false.

Example:
- Vision: create an AI tutor.
- Impact: help students learn independently.
- Relationship: the tutor is a proposed means; independent learning is the intended outcome.

### Impact and Interest
A person may be attracted to an activity without caring strongly about its broader outcome. Another person may care deeply about an outcome but have little attraction to the work currently associated with it.

Interest in an activity and concern for its consequences are related but distinct.

### Impact and Skill
Desired impact can exceed current capacity. A person may want to improve diagnosis in hospitals while lacking medical, technical, or research expertise. This indicates a possible capacity gap and may also reveal a need for collaboration.

Existing skills may make certain impacts more attainable or reveal new outcomes the person could pursue.

### Impact and Resources
Impact often shapes which resources are relevant. The resources needed to build a prototype may differ from those needed to deploy it safely or reach a large population.

Resources influence the scale, method, and feasibility of an intended outcome; they do not by themselves establish whether the outcome matters to the person.

## 5. Key Distinction

**Impact is the change or result the person wants to bring about—not merely the thing they want to do.** Vision and impact can be connected without being identical, and either may be clear while the other remains unknown.
